## HIER-033 — finite count-funded operator bank

Independent verdict: accepted as a valid bounded negative selector assay and counterexample bank.
Every fixture, packet, prediction and all 270 base/edited/recovered fields regenerate; CPU
replays are bit-exact. Raw objectives differ only by <=5.42e-20 under independent arithmetic,
PSNR by <=1.42e-14, and MS-SSIM/LPIPS match exactly.

The frozen mixed selector reaches joint immediate-and-recovered normalized regret <=0.1 in
13/18 constructed cases (72.22%), below the required >=80% (15/18). Immediate-only succeeds in
14/18; recovered-only in 16/18. Failures: width0, rotation0/1/2 and two_lobes2.
The [complete decision](archive/hier033/decision.json) retains all finite-bank alternatives.

Nine width/rotation/color cases have position activity >1e-8 and coherence <0.01 while a
continuous edit beats every donor-funded split plus no-op immediately. Two-lobe cases show that
similarly low coherence can instead favor a funded split. Cancellation therefore does not uniquely
identify edit family in this bank. The three support-gap fixtures have zero existing-row gradients
while a residual-seeded funded birth wins both immediate and recovered phases.

Recovery changes rankings: width0's selected split loses immediately but becomes the recovered
oracle; two_lobes2's immediate split winner fails recovered regret. A recovered gain includes
ordinary fitting: an edit-specific benefit must compare against equally recovered no-op.

Accounting: 6,318 complete Gaussian renders, 5,436 autograd backwards and 18 analytic packets,
excluding perceptual-network work. Each recovery charges 21 renders and 20 backwards.
All edits preserve N=3 and price actual donor deletion. Angle and donor cost co-vary across the
three conditions; these are constructed mechanisms, not independent random scenes. A negative
mixed selector does not refute every gradient-based controller or any general 3D splitting theorem.
Higher-order/support mechanisms were not isolated.
